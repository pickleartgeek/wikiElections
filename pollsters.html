Pollster Ratings

Every outlet that has published a race poll, graded on how close it landed to the logged result. CENSUS and ELECTION are the official series and are not graded.

Loading…

How grades work

Each poll is compared with the first-round result among the candidates it polled (or the runoff result for a head-to-head poll, when one exists). A poll scores out of 100: 70% for how close its shares were to the result (average miss in points, 25 points off or worse scores zero) and 30% for naming the right leader.

A pollster's grade is the weighted average of its poll scores — polls released closer to election day count more, and older polls fade with a one-year half-life — pulled toward a C+ with the weight of two imaginary polls, so one lucky or unlucky poll cannot decide a grade. Pollsters with fewer than three graded polls are marked provisional. Polls for races with no logged result yet are listed but not graded.

Variants of one outlet (e.g. “self-poll” or “H2H” editions) count together.
